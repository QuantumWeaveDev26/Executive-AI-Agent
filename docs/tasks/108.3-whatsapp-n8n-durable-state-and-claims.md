## Storage and security design

- Four additive tables: owner-bound connections (binding/revision/tombstone), inbound messages (recipient/conversation reference), dispatches (exact draft/hash/approval/expiry/claim/receipt), status events (deduplication and early correlation).
- Every business primary key and connection foreign key includes workspace, owner and connection. Global user/approval/audit references are additionally checked against the scoped owner/tenant. Unique idempotency/provider/event keys are designed to persist across process restarts. No credentials in these tables.
- Forced RLS derives tenant/owner/role from the authenticated session, not payload scope settings. No worker/public access. App queries repeat workspace/owner predicates. Production route/service-principal admission is deliberately not enabled.
- A transaction locks the connection row before reading or changing related state. Approval rows are locked when resolved. Fresh authenticated scope is checked before publication. Connection-level serialization is intentionally conservative for the bounded test-number pilot.
- Claim is single-use, expires after 60 seconds, rechecks exact policy approval and recipient/window/reply/revision binding. Claim never enables sending. Unknown outcomes cannot release/recreate a claim or reservation.
- Audit writes and state changes share the same transaction; errors roll back both. Disconnect increments the durable revision and retains a tombstone and deduplication/receipt evidence. No automatic reconnect or retry.
- Existing memory bridge remains a labelled synthetic fixture. New durable service has no network transport or runtime registration. Future LIVE admission requires a separately reviewed callback principal, secret store and transport.
